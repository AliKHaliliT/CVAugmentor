# 0033. Budget the em dash and name the prose tells

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-08-28

## Context

The prose law was two hard bans and a gesture. The clause-colon splice
was banned, the em dash was banned absolutely, and everything else
machine writing gets wrong hid inside "softer language-model tells" with
three examples. Nearly every byte of this family's prose is
machine-written, so the law most needed here was the one left vaguest.
Reading the public repository petergyang/no-ai-slop, an editing skill
built on a named catalog of AI writing patterns, showed the missing form,
since a named pattern is checkable where a vibe is not.

The owner also reversed the em-dash ban. The character earns its place
where it clearly beats the punctuation it replaces, and the real disease
is the cluster, so an absolute ban traded away legitimate uses to prevent
a plague that a count can prevent instead.

## Decision

The rulebook gains a Prose section, one text across the family and held
by the family audit. It carries the house voice with its animate idiom
kept and its vague actors banned, the flourish census, the splice ban,
the em-dash budget, the portability test, the inflated-vocabulary and
padding-phrase families, source-or-die attribution, the protected fact,
the plain verb, the theater catalog with its delete-never-rewrite remedy
for profound kickers, the formatting law, and a closing self-check the
writer runs before shipping. The words are the family's own, rewritten
from judgment rather than transplanted, because borrowed prose teaches a
borrowed voice.

The em dash is budgeted rather than banned. It is legal where it clearly
beats the comma, the parenthesis, or the period it replaces, it spends
its paragraph's one flourish, and a tracked file carries at most two.

Gates follow the family's two tiers. CI counts the per-file em-dash
budget, a boundary a count can settle. A grep over the domain-safe subset
of the vocabulary advises and never gates, excluding the rulebooks, the
records, and the treasury, where such words are legitimately quoted.
Everything else in the section is review's, and the section says so.

## Options considered

- Keeping the absolute em-dash ban was refused by the owner, since the
  ban solved a cluster problem by outlawing the legitimate single use.
- A verdict grep on the vocabulary was refused, because a banned word can
  be an honest domain term, and a check may never imply more than it
  decides.
- The source's voice-preservation machinery was refused as machinery and
  kept as one inverted idea, that the house voice is the voice an editing
  pass must not flatten.
- The source's active-voice absolutism was scoped, because this family
  deliberately animates its machinery, and only the vague actor that
  hides a decision is banned.

## Consequences

Every record written under the old ban carries zero em dashes and stays
untouched, since records are history. The advisory will sometimes fire on
honest domain prose, which is its design, advice for review rather than a
verdict. The Prose section rides the rulebook into every derived project,
and the self-check gives the writing agent its gate at the moment of
writing, where the fix is cheapest.
